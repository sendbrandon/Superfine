# [SERIES NAME] — Series Bible

A show built from the DNA of the DoorDash argument clip. Not the clip on
repeat. The clip's directing becomes the house style. The clip's two people
become two of four characters. The clip itself becomes the cold open of the
pilot. Everything else is new.

The unit of production is the **scene**: one 15-second generation. An episode
is five to eight scenes cut together. The DIRECTOR block is pasted verbatim at
the top of every scene prompt. The CAST cards are pasted for whoever is in the
scene. Only the scene changes.

Character handles (DOT, FLEECE, CANVAS, DAGGER) are working handles taken from
the wardrobe. Replace with real names everywhere once chosen. Roles are
proposals; overwrite them.

---

## 1. The show

**Premise.** Four broke roommates in a falling-apart apartment. Not broke and
sad. Broke and litigious. Every episode is one petty principle pushed all the
way to the end, with survival stakes underneath it. The apartment is home
base. The show leaves it constantly: the fire escape, the bodega, the
laundromat, the landlord, the job, the bus, the ER, the party.

**Engine.** Someone violates a rule of living together. The crew prosecutes.
The trial spreads through the day and out into the city. The verdict lands on
the wrong person.

**Season spine.** The power gets cut in the pilot. The season is the four of
them trying to get it back on without paying for it, one scheme per episode,
while the standalone trials keep happening in the dark. Finale: the power
comes back. The apartment in full light for the first time. It is disgusting.
They turn it off.

**Format.** 9:16 vertical. Episodes of 75 to 120 seconds, built from 15-second
scenes. Cold open is the crime. Last scene is the verdict. Every scene ends on
a face, which is also the cut point.

## 2. DIRECTOR (paste verbatim at the top of every scene prompt)

> Photoreal live-action, 9:16 vertical, 15 seconds, five to seven quick cuts. Handheld with slight micro-shake. Faces played straight into the lens: when a character speaks to someone, they speak INTO the camera, the lens is the other person, wide-ish lens up close so hands and faces distort big in the foreground, shallow depth of field. One wide shot per scene to show the room and the bodies, then back to faces. One dominant light source per scene, whatever the location gives, deep falloff, teal-green shadows, muted color, fine film grain, slight halation on the source. No music, no captions, no titles, room tone and dialogue only. Tone: Rick and Morty meets Larry David meets Patrice O'Neal. Raw, vulgar, cutting comedy played dead serious at full intensity. Nobody winks.

Where it comes from: the clip's extreme close-ups played into the lens, the
one wide that resets the room, the single pendant bulb doing all the lighting,
the warm-amber-over-teal grade, the grain, the dead-serious performance.
Generalized so it works in a bodega, on a bus, in a courtroom.

## 6. Scene rules (the filter)

1. **Every scene is one petty principle with survival stakes underneath.** If the concept arrives without stakes, add the bill.
2. **Nobody argues about the thing.** They argue the principle behind the thing, and the principle becomes a worldview inside four seconds. (Rick and Morty.)
3. **Every insult is a diagnosis.** About the other person's habits, history, and body of work in this apartment. If it could be said to a stranger, cut it. (Patrice O'Neal.)
4. **Evidence in frame.** The crime is a physical object and the wide shows it.
5. **One light per scene.** It changes when the location changes. Never two sources.
6. **End on a face.** That is the cut point to the next scene.
7. **Dead serious.** Nobody knows it is funny. No smirks, no winks, no bits.
8. **Four in the room is still a two-hander.** The other two are witnesses. One of them gets the button.
9. **The verdict lands on the wrong person.** (Larry David.)

### Scene 2 — THE DARK

```
[DIRECTOR, verbatim]

CAST. @Image1 is DOT: late-20s woman, long platinum-blonde wavy hair, pale freckled skin, grey-blue eyes, oversized petrol-blue sweatshirt with a small orange oval patch, cream silk neckerchief with red polka dots, khaki cargo trousers, rust-red canvas tote on her shoulder. @Image2 is FLEECE: early-30s man, brown skin, short bleached-platinum curls, thin mustache and goatee, gold hoop earring, oversized black sherpa fleece hoodie, dark baggy jeans, chunky black boots. @Image3 is CANVAS: late-20s man, buzzed strawberry-blond head, ruddy freckled skin, ginger mustache and chin scruff, faded tan canvas hooded work jacket with a black metal-font print, white tee, baggy light-wash jeans. @Image4 is DAGGER: mid-20s man, long dirty-blond hair to the shoulders, sparse mustache, gaunt, black cap with an embroidered crest, oversized sky-blue acid-wash hoodie with a grey dagger and red gothic lettering, plaid flannel tied at the waist, grey acid-wash baggy jeans.

SCENE. The kitchen, the second the power dies. Total black, then phones. The only light in the scene is phone flashlights and phone screens, cold white and blue, faces lit from below, everything else black. Peeling paint, the filthy sink, stacks of bills, the DoorDash bag on the counter.

BEAT. The bulb just died mid-fight. DOT and CANVAS light their phones and find each other's faces and keep going. FLEECE walks in from the hallway holding his phone up like a torch, calm, and asks why it's a séance in here. DAGGER has been sitting on the counter in the dark the whole time, eating the chips. He knew. The shut-off notice is under his cup.

SHOTS.
1. Black frame for one second. DOT's phone flashlight snaps on under her face, ECU, mid-word, still furious.
2. ECU CANVAS lit blue by his own screen, direct to lens: that was the last bulb and this is what she wanted.
3. WIDE. Four phone lights in a black kitchen: DOT left, CANVAS right, FLEECE in the doorway with his light held high, DAGGER on the counter between them, the chip bag glowing.
4. ECU FLEECE direct to lens, lit from below, half-smile, the séance line.
5. ECU DAGGER, chewing, lit by his phone, direct to lens: they said noon.
6. Insert: DAGGER lifts his cup off the counter, DOT's flashlight finds the shut-off notice under it, a ring stain on the date.
7. ECU DOT, mouth open, nothing comes out.

DIALOGUE. Rapid, overlapping, four to seven exchanges. Anchors: FLEECE: "Why is it a séance in here." DAGGER: "They said noon." Insults are diagnoses of each other's habits and history, vulgar and cutting, not overdone. Nobody is aware it's funny.
```

### Scene 3 — THE PLAN

```
[DIRECTOR, verbatim]

CAST. [all four card lines]

SCENE. The living room, same night. The only light is the streetlight outside the window, sodium orange through dirty glass, laying one hard rectangle across the floor. BEAT. FLEECE has a plan and he is calm about it: run an extension cord out the window, down the fire escape, into the outlet behind the laundromat downstairs. It's not stealing, it's a shared grid. SHOTS.

### Scene 5 — THE BODEGA

```
[DIRECTOR, verbatim]

```

### Scene 6 — THE VERDICT

```
[DIRECTOR, verbatim]

CAST. [all four card lines] A delivery driver in the doorway, any age, phone in hand.

SCENE. The kitchen, midnight. The only light is six tall glass saint candles in a row on the counter, warm flicker from below, black beyond arm's reach. The DoorDash burrito from the cold open, cut into four on a plate between the candles.

BEAT. Peace. The four eat the burrito in quarters in silence, the first quiet moment of the episode. Then a knock. 4. ECU the driver in the doorway, lit by his own phone, reading: order for DOT?
5. ECU CANVAS direct to lens, slow, the stillness before the eruption.
6. ECU DOT direct to lens, every muscle in her face trying to build a defense and failing. Cut to black on her face.

DIALOGUE. Almost none until the knock. Anchors: DRIVER: "Order for [DOT]?" DOT says nothing. CANVAS: "You ordered first." Black. Nobody winks.

## 9. Production notes

- **One scene, one generation.** Same four reference images, same order, same `@Image` numbers, every time.
- **Cut points.** Every scene ends on a face. Cut on the face. When the location does not change between scenes, take the last frame of the previous scene as the first-frame reference of the next.
- **Dialogue.** Anchors plus improv. Two or three exact lines per scene at most, the model performs the rest. Go fully scripted only when a scene needs exact words.
- **Sound.** Room tone and dialogue in the generation. Music, title card, and any captions happen in the edit.
- **Reference syntax.** `@Image1` is Dreamina / Seedance 2.0. Swap for the tool's syntax if generating elsewhere.
