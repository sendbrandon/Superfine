# EyeCandy Vocabulary — techniques as physics

Every technique from eyecannndy.com, translated into a sentence the video
model can act on. The name is for us. The sentence is what goes in the
prompt. Numbers are starting values, change them per scene.

Rules:
- Two to four techniques per scene, never more. Each one gets one clause.
- Always say what does not move alongside what does.
- (house) = already part of the show's directing. (pick) = strongest fits
  for our scene types: arguments, transformations, reveals, buttons.

---

## The house style, in EyeCandy terms

What the DoorDash clip is doing, named: **Fourth Wall** (played straight
into the lens), **Close-Up**, **Handheld**, **Quick Cuts**, **Two Shot** in
**Profile** for the wide, **Hard Light** from one source, **Halation** on
it, **Shallow Focus**, **Vignette** from the falloff. Every scene prompt
already carries these. Add techniques on top, don't replace them.

## Starter picks for this show

Transformation, Morphing, Speed Ramp, Whip Pan, Crash Cut, Crash Zoom,
Split Diopter, Dolly Zoom, Snorricam, Object POV, Pass Through, Freeze
Frame, Dutch Angle, Worms-Eye, Match Cut, Cut-Ins, Scale Shift, Duplication.

## Camera position and framing

- **Aerial.** Camera 50 meters straight up or angled 45 degrees down, people are dots, streets are lines, drifting sideways at 2 meters per second.
- **Central Framing.** Subject dead center on the vertical axis, background symmetrical left and right, camera level, nothing off-center.
- **Close-Up.** (house) Face fills 80 percent of the frame, top of the head cut off, chin at the bottom edge, lens within 60 centimeters.
- **First-Person.** Camera is the character's eyes at 1.6 meters, their own hands enter from the bottom corners, the frame bobs 2 centimeters with each step.
- **Fixed Cam.** Camera locked on a tripod, zero movement for the whole shot, action enters and leaves the frame on its own.
- **Fourth Wall.** (house) The character looks and speaks straight down the lens as if the camera is the person they're talking to.
- **Ground Level.** Lens 5 centimeters off the floor, the floor fills the bottom half of the frame, feet and wheels are giant, faces are far away.
- **High Angle.** Camera 30 degrees above the eye line looking down, the subject shrinks, the floor around them is visible.
- **Low Angle.** Camera at knee height tilted up 20 degrees, the subject towers, the ceiling is visible behind their head.
- **Object POV.** (pick) The camera is mounted on or inside the object: the lens is the bottle, the tote, the bulb, and the world moves around it.
- **Over the Shoulder.** Camera 40 centimeters behind one character's shoulder, that shoulder soft in the left third of frame, the other person's face sharp.
- **Overhead.** Camera pointing straight down, 90 degrees, 2 to 3 meters above, bodies flattened into shapes on the floor.
- **Profile.** (house) Faces side-on, 90 degrees to the lens, noses pointing left and right at each other.
- **Two Shot.** (house) Two people in one frame at equal size sharing the width.
- **Wide Shot.** Full bodies head to toe with air above and below, the room's geometry visible.
- **Ultra Wide.** 14 millimeter lens, the edges of the frame stretch, straight lines bow outward, a whole room in one frame from its corner.
- **Worms-Eye.** (pick) Lens on the ground pointing straight up, the subject and the ceiling above it, feet nearest the lens and largest.
- **Voyeur.** Camera hidden behind a doorframe, blinds, or a window, a soft obstruction covering 30 percent of the frame, the subject unaware.
- **Tableau.** Everyone frozen in a posed wide, camera locked, one composition held for the whole shot, only breathing moves.
- **Interview.** Subject seated, three-quarter to the lens, eyes to an off-camera interviewer, clean background, static camera.
- **Video Portraits.** A held static portrait of one person doing almost nothing for the whole shot, blinking, breathing, shifting once.
- **Silhouette.** Subject pure black against a bright background, zero fill light, only the edge of the body reads.
- **Spotlight.** (house) One hard circle of light 1 meter across on the subject, everything outside the circle black.
- **Dutch Angle.** (pick) Camera rolled 15 degrees on the lens axis so the horizon slants, held for the shot; increase the angle with each cut to escalate.

## Camera movement

- **Arc.** Camera circles the subject on a 2 meter radius through 180 degrees at constant speed, the subject stays centered, the background wheels behind.
- **Bolt Cam.** A robotic arm whips the camera 2 meters in half a second and stops dead, while the subject is in slow motion.
- **Camera Roll.** Camera rotates around the lens axis 180 degrees over 2 seconds, the horizon goes vertical then upside down.
- **Dolly.** Camera on wheels moving forward on a straight line at 0.5 meters per second, lens fixed, perspective changes.
- **Dolly Zoom.** (pick) Camera pushes in while the lens zooms out so the subject's face stays the same size and the background stretches away behind them, 2 seconds.
- **Double Dolly.** Subject and camera on the same moving platform, they move together, the background slides past behind them.
- **FPV Drone.** Camera flies through gaps and under things at 10 meters per second, banking 30 degrees, one continuous shot, no cuts.
- **Handheld.** (house) Slight shake, 1 to 2 centimeters of drift, breathing in the frame.
- **Lazy Susan.** Subject on a turntable rotating a full 360 degrees in 4 seconds, camera locked.
- **Locked-On.** Camera rigidly attached to the subject so they stay perfectly still in frame while the world jerks around them.
- **Omnidirectional.** A 360-degree camera look, the whole environment wrapped around the subject, extreme warp at the edges.
- **Pan.** Camera rotates horizontally on a fixed point, 45 degrees over 2 seconds.
- **Parallax.** Camera moves sideways and three depth layers slide at three speeds, foreground fastest, background slowest.
- **Pass Through.** (pick) Camera moves through a solid thing or an opening, a keyhole, a wall, a mouth, and comes out in another space.
- **Pedestal.** Camera rises straight up 1 meter in 2 seconds, no tilt, the subject slides down the frame.
- **Probe.** Macro lens on a long thin barrel moving through tiny spaces, inside a bag, between fingers, 2 centimeters from the surface.
- **Snorricam.** (pick) Camera rigged to the actor's chest facing them, their face locked dead center, the background swinging as they walk.
- **Tilt.** Camera pivots vertically on a fixed point, up 30 degrees in 2 seconds.
- **Tracking.** Camera moves alongside a moving subject at their speed, keeping them in the same spot in frame.
- **Trucking.** Camera slides sideways on a line parallel to the subject at 1 meter per second.
- **Wandering.** Camera drifts through a space with no subject, 0.3 meters per second, looking around as if searching.
- **Whip Pan.** (pick) Camera whips 90 degrees in 4 frames, everything blurs into streaks, lands on a new subject.
- **Zoom.** Lens goes from 24 to 85 millimeters over 2 seconds, camera doesn't move, everything scales up.
- **Crash Zoom.** (pick) Same but violent, 24 to 200 millimeters in 3 frames, lands on one detail.
- **Falling.** Camera drops with the subject at gravity speed, the ground rushing up, 1 second, hair and clothes pulled upward.
- **Levitation.** Subject rises 30 centimeters off the ground and hangs, clothes and hair drifting, feet pointed down, nothing else moves.

## Lens and optics

- **Fisheye.** 8 millimeter circular lens, the world bends into a sphere, the center bulges toward the viewer, edges curve.
- **Focal Shift.** Focus racks from the foreground to the background in 1 second, one goes soft as the other goes sharp.
- **Shallow Focus.** (house) One plane 20 centimeters deep is sharp, everything in front of and behind it melts.
- **Split Diopter.** (pick) Two planes sharp at once, one face foreground left, the other 3 meters back on the right, a soft vertical seam between them.
- **Magnification.** A lens, a glass, or a drop of water in frame enlarges what's behind it 3 times.
- **Tilt Shift.** Only a horizontal band across the middle of the frame is sharp, above and below blur, everything looks like a miniature.
- **Halation.** (house) Light sources bloom with a warm halo, highlights bleed into the shadows around them.
- **Haze.** Smoke or dust hanging in the air, beams of light visible, contrast dropping with distance.
- **Vignette.** (house) Corners darken 40 percent, the eye is pulled to the center.
- **Reflections.** The subject seen in a mirror, a window, a puddle, or a black screen, the reflection carrying the shot.
- **Motion Blur.** Fast movement streaks across 20 pixels per frame, the shutter open long.
- **Distortions.** The image warps, ripples, and stretches like heat over a road, 2 centimeter waves crossing the frame.

## Editing and time

- **Quick Cuts.** (house) A cut every 1 to 2 seconds.
- **Crash Cut.** (pick) A hard cut mid-motion into a totally different shot, no transition, nothing warns you.
- **Cut-Ins.** (pick) 1-second insert shots of details, a hand, a receipt, a coin, between the main shots.
- **Flash Cut.** A 2-frame flash of a different image inside a shot, gone before it registers.
- **Jump Cut.** Same framing, time skips, the subject jumps 30 centimeters between frames.
- **Match Cut.** (pick) Cut from one shape to the same shape in another scene, a round bulb to a round moon, same position in frame.
- **Match Motion.** Cut from one movement to the same movement in a different scene, the swing continues across the cut.
- **Match Split.** Two shots share the frame, joined along a line where their shapes line up.
- **Speed Ramp.** (pick) Normal speed for 1 second, drops to 25 percent for 1 second, snaps back to normal.
- **Slow Motion.** 5 times slower, hair floats, drops hang, a blink takes a second.
- **Fast Motion.** 4 times faster, crowds blur, clouds race, one subject moves at normal speed inside it.
- **Freeze Frame.** (pick) Everything stops dead on one frame for 1 second, then resumes.
- **Stutter.** Each frame held 3 times, motion staggers like a skipping video.
- **Step-Print.** Every other frame doubled, motion smears in rhythm, a strobing drag behind movement.
- **Boomerang.** A 1-second action plays forward, backward, forward.
- **Infinite Loop.** The last frame matches the first exactly, the shot can play forever.
- **Cinemagraph.** A still photograph where one element moves, steam or hair or a flag, everything else frozen.
- **Zoetrope.** Motion built from a spinning ring of frames, a strobed sequence of poses going around.
- **Stop Motion.** Objects move 1 centimeter per frame with no motion blur, 12 frames per second, jittery.
- **Bullet Time.** Action frozen while the camera arcs 180 degrees around it, debris hanging in the air.
- **Transitions.** A device that gets from one shot to the next: a wipe, a dissolve, an object crossing the lens.
- **Set Transition.** The set changes around the subject in one continuous shot, walls slide away, the room becomes another room.
- **Ratio Switch.** The aspect ratio changes mid-scene, black bars move in or out.
- **Screen in Screen.** A phone or TV inside the frame plays its own video and we cut into it.
- **Split Screen.** Two or more panels at once, each its own shot, divided by a hard line.
- **Echo Print.** A moving subject leaves fading copies of itself behind, 4 ghosts spaced 10 centimeters apart.
- **Duplication.** (pick) The same person appears 3 times in one frame doing different things.
- **Feedback.** Camera pointed at its own output, a tunnel of the same image repeating smaller and smaller.
- **Datamosh.** Compression breaks, pixels from the last shot smear and drag over the new one in blocks.
- **Slit-Scan.** Time spread across the frame, the left side a second ahead of the right, bodies stretch and smear.
- **Wigglegram.** Two frames from lenses 6 centimeters apart alternate rapidly, a 3D wobble.

## Light

- **Hard Light.** (house) One small bright source, sharp-edged shadows, hot highlights, deep black falloff.
- **Light Flash.** A strobe pop for one frame, white-out, then normal.
- **Color Shift.** The whole image changes hue, warm to green over 2 seconds, or a color wash sweeping in from one side.
- **Night Vision.** Green monochrome, heavy grain, pupils glowing, black vignette.
- **Thermal.** Heat-map palette, faces white and orange, the room blue and black.
- **X-Ray.** Bones and objects visible through skin and walls, blue-white on black.
- **Shadow Box.** Subject lit inside a small box or frame, black all around, one lit cell in darkness.

## VFX and transformation

- **Morphing.** (pick) One thing's shape becomes another's over 2 seconds, edges flow, no cut.
- **Transformation.** (pick) A body or object changes state with physics, skin, scale, or material changing in a wave from one end to the other over a stated number of seconds.
- **Scale Shift.** (pick) Subject shrinks or grows 10 times in 2 seconds while the room stays the same, or the reverse.
- **Masking.** Part of the frame cut out in a shape and replaced with another image, a window inside the picture.
- **Object Portal.** An object's opening, a mouth, a bag, a keyhole, is a doorway to another place and the camera goes through it. Pairs with Pass Through.
- **Diorama.** The world as a tiny built model, visible seams, tilt-shift focus, a giant hand can enter from above.
- **Floating UI.** Interface elements hanging in the air 20 centimeters from the subject's face, bubbles, meters, windows.
- **Magical Realism.** One impossible thing in a real scene, treated as ordinary by everyone in it. (This is the Bigfoot rule.)
- **Anthropo.** Objects or animals with human faces, gestures, and posture.
- **Photogrammetry.** A 3D-scanned person or place with scan artifacts, holes in the mesh, floating textures.
- **Generative.** Procedural evolving patterns that respond to the subject's movement.
- **Projections.** Images projected onto the subject's face and body, wrapping around the contours.
- **Kaleidoscope.** The frame mirrored into 6 or 8 wedges, the subject repeated in a circle.
- **Double Exposure.** Two images at 50 percent each, a face and a landscape sharing one frame.
- **Collage.** Cut-paper layers with hard edges, elements at different scales pasted together.
- **Mixed Media.** Live action with animation, paper, or paint drawn on top of it.
- **Animation.** Hand-drawn or CG, frame-by-frame, stated style.
- **Pixel Art.** 8-bit grid, big square pixels, limited palette.
- **Video Game.** A game HUD, third-person camera behind the subject, health bar, respawn.
- **Typography.** Words as the visual. Not used on this show, no text in frame.
- **Stylistic Suck.** Deliberately bad on purpose: bad green screen, wrong lighting, the amateur look as a choice.

## World and mood

- **Altered State.** Warped visuals, breathing walls, saturated color, the image drifts like a hallucination.
- **Dreamcore.** Empty familiar spaces, soft light, too quiet, a hallway that doesn't end.
- **Weirdcore.** Low resolution, wrong colors, uncanny objects, a face where a face shouldn't be.
- **Dystopian.** Grey, concrete, surveillance cameras, uniforms, no plants.
- **Epiphany.** A slow push in on a face as it realizes something, the light changing on it.
- **Gesture.** A single hand or body movement is the whole shot.
- **Choreo.** Bodies moving in synchronized designed patterns.
- **Conveyor.** Subjects or objects moving past at constant speed on a belt or track while the camera stays.
- **Architexture.** Buildings as the subject, texture and geometry, no people.
- **Maximalism.** Every inch of frame filled, pattern on pattern, no negative space.
- **Void.** Subject alone in infinite black or white, no floor line, no walls.
- **Underwater.** Subject submerged, hair floating, bubbles rising 30 centimeters per second, light rays from above.
- **Vintage.** VHS or film damage, tracking lines, faded color, dust and scratches.
- **Product.** The object hero-lit on a clean surface, slow rotation, nothing else in frame.
- **Photography.** Stills with a shutter-click rhythm, a freeze on each click.
- **BTS.** The making-of look, crew, rigs, and lights visible in frame.
